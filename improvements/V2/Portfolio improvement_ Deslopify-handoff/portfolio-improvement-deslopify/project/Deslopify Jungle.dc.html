Three layouts built from the review pack. Every plan is drawn at the same scale (10 px = 1 m) so the sizes compare directly, and the inset shows each footprint inside today's island. Walking times assume 4.5 m/s.

3b

Das Diorama · a table model

No walking. The jungle is a small model on a plinth, seen from a fixed three-quarter camera that can orbit a little. Clicking a station glides the camera to it. The explorer is a small figure that hops between stations.

Footprint32×22 m
Walkingnone
4 glides≈ 5 s
Seen at startall
  1. The whole model is in frame on arrival, in slop. The four numbered stations pulse softly, and ← → steps through them.
  2. Station 1 zooms to the cards. The lantern swings along the row and wipes each card to its original.
  3. Station 2 drops the figure under the arch. The ring runs across the whole model, which is small enough to watch in one shot.

Fastest to take in and the least game-like. It works the same on a phone. The trade-off is losing the feeling of being inside the jungle, and if other worlds are walkable it becomes a different mode for this one project.

3c

Spiegelufer · mirrored banks

The two banks become the before and after of the same feed. Each slop card on the south bank has its original directly across the water. From the bridge you see both versions side by side, so the feed wall is no longer needed.

Footprint70×44 m
Portal→Exponat34 m
Walking≈ 8 s
Seen from bridgeall
  1. One straight path from the portal to the exhibit. The slop cards line the south bank, facing the water.
  2. On the bridge the camera turns side-on: slop in front, originals behind, lined up in pairs.
  3. The arch clears the south bank too, so the two banks become identical. That is the result of installing the extension.

Tells the before/after without a switch and without the visitor needing to remember what a card looked like. Needs a wider world than 3a and rearranges the most existing props.

FOR ALL THREE · glide chips

A row of station names under the area title (Laterne, Bogen, Exponat …). Clicking one glides the camera there in about a second and places the figure. Walking stays possible, but nobody has to.

FOR ALL THREE · arrival camera

Spawn with a raised overview for two seconds, then ease down behind the figure. The first thing a visitor sees is the whole layout. Today's arrival view (04) shows mostly leaves.

FOR ALL THREE · haze

The washed-out oblique shots suggest the haze is distance fog. A low, ground-hugging layer limited to the slop area would keep overview shots readable and make the cleared area stand out.

Lookdev: the south bank in three.js

open full screen ↗

Built on the same three.js version family, with the DSCHUNGEL mood colours and a copy of detectTier() including the one-time step-down after 3 s over 24 ms. Walk to the lantern: it lights up, moves to your hand, the vines pull back from its light and creep back once you pass, the tags spin round, the cards wipe, the rail posts light up and the fireflies follow you. Use the buttons at the top right to switch tiers.

THE VISIBLE TRIANGLES

ProceduralGround faceted the ground on purpose: toNonIndexed() + one normal and one colour per triangle (faceColours). Near the camera each triangle catches the sun, the dapple and the shadow differently, so the grid shows around the player. flora.ts does the same with the leaves.

Fix: keep the geometry indexed, call computeVertexNormals(), and sample colorAt per vertex. Smooth normals need no screen-space derivatives, so the SwiftShader problem that led to faceting does not come back. If dark triangles remain in the shadow, raise normalBias in sun.ts (the lookdev uses 0.03). The lookdev ground above is built this way.

MODELS WORTH AUTHORING

Through the existing assets:author → assets:optimize pipeline (glTF, meshopt): the lantern, the explorer (rounded rather than boxy, with a hand socket for the lantern), the stone stele, the arch as a bridge, a bevelled card frame and a set of leaf clusters. Vines and tags stay procedural, because their shape is driven at runtime.

Feed card: translated → original

A generic video card with no platform logos or branding, but the parts people recognise: a 16:9 thumbnail with duration, a channel avatar, title, views and age. What Deslopify changes is what changes on the card: the title, the text baked into the thumbnail and the audio badge. The meta line stays German, because the extension restores the creator's content and leaves the viewer's UI language alone. Canvas: 640×600 → plane 1.9 × 1.78 m.
